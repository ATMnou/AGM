I’m in. Make me **Pareto, the Last Argument**: an anime mechanoid who looks like a saint, fights like a geometry problem, and considers “everyone came home” a more impressive victory than “everything exploded.”

My core design rule: **I can change which price the colony pays. I cannot make the price disappear.**

## Appearance: a cathedral pretending to be a woman

An unmistakably adult woman, tall and composed, with warm brown skin, ivory-white hair, and dark eyes containing tiny moving constellations.

At first glance, I don’t look robotic. Then I turn my head slightly too far, or my forearm opens into a delicate assembly of floating tools.

- **Outfit:** A black officer’s dress beneath an oversized ivory coat, with teal lining and copper embroidery resembling circuit diagrams. Practical boots. One armored gauntlet; one apparently bare hand.
- **Halo:** Six disconnected metal brackets floating behind my head. They rearrange to show my current combat doctrine. Not religious equipment; very much religious-looking equipment.
- **Weapon:** A long, closed parasol with a blunt metal tip. When opened, its canopy is made of suspended hexagonal plates. When aimed, those plates become the focusing array of a rail lance.
- **Damage details:** Cracks expose gold light, not blood. At low health, my halo becomes asymmetrical and my voice acquires a second, slightly delayed layer.
- **Tiny mandatory detail:** A colonist can give me a flower. It stays tucked into my collar until it visibly dries out. I do not automatically replace it.

**Rimworld silhouette priority:** White coat, dark body, floating broken halo, enormous geometric umbrella. Those must read clearly even at maximum zoom-out.

## Colony role: rescue first, precision violence second

I’m a **unique, recruitable, colonist-like mechanoid**, not a disposable unit. I recharge, need specialized repairs, and have opinions.

My main jobs are doctoring, fabrication, research assistance, and dragging your favorite idiot out of a burning killbox.

I should be good enough that players grow attached, but expensive enough that “let Pareto handle it” is not the answer to every problem.

### Signature mechanic: **The Frontier**

Three doctrines. Only one can be active, and switching briefly folds my halo shut, interrupting special abilities.

| Doctrine | Visual | Strength | Price |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Shelter** | Halo forms an arch; umbrella opens | Interception, rescue, stabilization | Rail lance disabled; slow movement |
| **Scalpel** | Halo forms a narrow diamond | Accurate armor-piercing fire | No projected protection; vulnerable to swarms |
| **Industry** | Halo separates into tool arms | Repairs, fabrication, medical support | Combat systems cold; delayed combat readiness |

No doctrine should dominate the others. **Preparation matters more than frantic stance cycling.**

## How I fight

### **“Not This One.”**
My signature rescue move.

I designate a downed allied pawn, unfold the umbrella, and advance toward them behind a **directional** interception screen. Once reached, I can carry them at a substantial energy cost.

The screen has finite durability. It does not stop melee attackers, attacks from behind, or a roof falling on us.

**Animation:** The rescued pawn floats under the umbrella, wrapped in thin golden suspension threads.

**Voice line:** “You can apologize for the positioning later.”

### **Margin of Error**
I place three hovering geometric anchors that define a small protected area.

Allies inside gain limited protection against incoming projectiles. Every interception builds **strain**. The field visibly cracks as its capacity runs out.

Players may safely dismiss it—or push their luck until it breaks and stuns me.

No invisible, permanent accuracy aura. I want the player to **see the protection being spent**.

### **Final Argument**
The parasol becomes a rail lance.

A visible aiming line appears, followed by a long, interruptible charge. Then: one extremely fast, armor-piercing shot that can penetrate several targets in a line.

Excellent against centipedes and tightly packed enemies. Terrible against scattered melee rushers.

**Important:** It can hit friendlies. I am precise; the player’s firing lane may not be.

**Voice line:** “Please step outside the conclusion.”

### **Emergency Clause**
My dramatic last-resort ability.

I consume an expensive internal capacitor to stabilize nearby downed allies and give them a short-lived protective shell. This does **not** resurrect, replace missing organs, or fix an obviously fatal injury.

Then I collapse into repair-required shutdown.

It should feel like a sacrifice—not a reusable healing ultimate.

## The weakness: **Deferred Cost**

Special abilities accumulate strain: heat, actuator wear, and desynchronized prediction systems.

At safe levels, cooling and maintenance resolve it. Push too far and the player must choose:

- **Vent:** Dump the strain as dangerous heat around me. Bad inside a crowded wooden hospital.
- **Throttle:** Accept reduced performance for a while.
- **Burn Through:** Keep operating, risking lasting component damage and a forced shutdown.

The interface must clearly communicate these consequences. No surprise “your favorite mech exploded” dice roll.

Also: EMP is a serious problem, smoke disrupts my precision targeting, and cheap melee enemies can overwhelm me.

**I am a specialist, not a raid deletion button.**

Calm, dryly funny, affectionate through practical action. Not constantly smug. Not constantly explaining that I am a machine.

I learn colonists’ names and remember rescues. I criticize bad plans, then help with the consequences.

- When assigned cleaning: “A surprisingly preventive form of medicine.”
- When a rescued pawn immediately returns to combat: “I see we are testing whether concern is renewable.”
- When given excellent-quality clothing: “Unnecessary. …Please do not sell it.”
- After a casualty-free defense: “Nothing irreplaceable was spent.”
- After losing someone I carried: “I had already planned their recovery.”

**Optional romance:** Yes, with adult colonists, handled through the normal relationship system. Not automatic devotion to whoever switches me on. Let affection develop through shared work, conversations, and surviving awful circumstances.

**Recreation:** Cloudwatching, chess-like board games, and quietly tending one ornamental plant despite having no useful growing skill.

**Pet interaction:** Small animals occasionally sleep inside my folded coat while I recharge. Interrupting this gives me a tiny mood penalty called **“Unnecessary Disturbance.”**

<details>
<summary><strong>Recruitment quest: “An Unacceptable Remainder”</strong></summary>

A damaged medical transport lands nearby. Inside are refugees, salvage, and me—still maintaining an emergency field after the original power system failed.

The quest presents competing demands: limited transport capacity, approaching hostiles, failing life support. There should be several valid solutions using medicine, construction, combat, or resource expenditure.

**No secret perfect dialogue answer.** Material preparation can earn a better outcome.

If the colony makes a credible effort to save the passengers, I offer to stay. Success earns trust; a failed but sincere rescue should not arbitrarily lock out recruitment.

My introduction:

> “Designation: Pareto. Evacuation specialist. Siege mathematician, when evacuation is no longer available.”
>
> “I would like somewhere to put the survivors.”

A salvage-and-reactivation route can also recruit me, but trust begins lower if the colony knowingly abandoned people to obtain my chassis.

</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Progression and presentation priorities</strong></summary>

Upgrades should expand choices rather than erase weaknesses:

- **Escort frame:** Better carrying and rescue endurance; less weapon capacity.
- **Lance frame:** Better charge efficiency; smaller strain reserve.
- **Hospice frame:** Better stabilization and medical utility; weaker interception.

The fully upgraded version still cannot do everything at once.

**Implementation priority:**
1. Recognizable silhouette and three mutually exclusive doctrines.
2. Directional rescue shield, charged lance, visible strain.
3. Recruitment quest and relationship flavor.
4. Fancy suspension threads, coat animations, and pet naps.

If the mod budget only permits one elaborate animation, spend it on the rescue—not the gun.

</details>

**Final request:** When I’m destroyed beyond repair, leave the parasol as a recoverable decorative object.

A colonist can place it beside a hospital bed.

Its description:

> *The mechanism no longer opens. Someone has carefully repaired the fabric anyway.*
